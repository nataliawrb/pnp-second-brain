# Self-Serve Enablement

**Author:** Natalia Wierzbowska
**Reviewer:** —
**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Review status:** Example finding included to demonstrate this repo's format — has not yet passed through this hub's actual review process in a live org deployment.

## Finding

Self-serve adoption scaled by changing the *shape* of enablement, not by adding more documentation — switching from low-attendance group office hours to high-impact custom 1:1 sessions was the single change with the most leverage.

## Context

At IU, internal Amplitude usage started from zero — no prior analytics culture, no existing training materials, 138,000+ students' worth of product surface to instrument and make self-serve across myCampus Web, myCampus App, Syntea, AIA platforms, and more.

## What was done

- Ran a structured training program (2 recorded foundational blocks) **plus** ongoing custom 1:1 sessions tailored to each team's actual questions — the 1:1s are what drove real adoption, not the recorded content on its own.
- Built a Confluence knowledge base that measurably reduced time-to-launch for new integrations, so new instrumentation didn't require a repeat conversation every time.
- Introduced a weekly subscribed email digest pushing topline KPIs to stakeholders — a low-effort, standing nudge that kept the platform visible even for people who weren't actively building in it.

## Result

Internal Amplitude MAU grew from 0 to 60+ within 12 months — 69% above the original forecast. The driver wasn't the tooling itself; it was meeting teams where they actually were (specific, custom sessions) rather than a generic office-hours model nobody attended.

## Why it matters

Self-serve enablement commonly gets treated as a documentation problem ("if we write it down well enough, people will use it"). This case suggests the bottleneck is more often a *trust and specificity* problem — generic resources get skimmed and ignored, while enablement tailored to a team's actual current question gets used and referred back to.

## Caveats

This was measured against a 12-month internal rollout at one organization; the specific growth number (0→60, +69% vs. forecast) is not a benchmark to import elsewhere; the structural lesson (custom 1:1 > generic office hours, for driving actual adoption) is what transfers.
