# Governance & Operating Model

How the function itself runs — sequencing, team shape, decision rights. This is the layer that determines whether product analytics scales past one person's calendar.

## Operating model & sequencing of work
What gets built first and why. Fundamentals before reporting, reporting before AI-native measurement — sequencing mistakes here compound everywhere downstream.

## How to grow the team / embedding
Whether analysts sit centrally or get embedded in product teams, and how that choice trades off consistency against proximity to the problem.

## How to avoid being a helpdesk
The default failure mode of an under-leveraged analytics function: every question routes through one person instead of through self-serve tools, documentation, or (increasingly) a knowledge hub a local Claude environment can check first. See [`knowledge-hub-second-brain.md`](knowledge-hub-second-brain.md).

## Interactions with product backlog & tracking plan
Where measurement planning actually plugs into the PRD/backlog process — ideally early (sizing, expected metric impact, a measurement plan), not bolted on after a feature ships.

## Prioritization techniques
How analytics initiatives themselves get prioritized against each other, not just how analytics informs product prioritization.

## How you decide on the stack
The criteria for adopting or changing a tool in the stack — not vendor preference, but fit to the org's actual maturity and existing data architecture.

## Legacy tracking: what didn't work, and deprecation strategy
Naming clearly what existed before, why it failed (lack of ownership, inconsistent standards, tooling mismatch), and how it gets sunset without breaking continuity on metrics people already rely on.

## Redefining the role of analyst / skill codification / no silos
The shift from "the analyst knows the answer" to "the analyst's thinking is encoded somewhere reusable" — skills, specs, this repo. No piece of organizational knowledge should be dependent on one person's presence.

## Expectations on documentation
What "documented" actually means in practice — enough that someone who wasn't in the room can act on it correctly.

## Audit method
See the root [`README.md`](../README.md) for the full audit method (interview → synthesize → understand domain overlap → evaluate against a standard → build a 30/60/90 roadmap) — it's the entry point for applying this operating model to a new org.
